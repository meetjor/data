# The Note in the Library Book

Leo went to the college library because he needed a quiet place to prepare for an exam. He chose a book about study methods and opened it at a random page.

A small note fell onto the desk.

It said:

> **If you are reading this, look under the clock near the window.**

Leo became **curious**. He wanted to know who had written the note and why. At first, he **hesitated**. He had planned to study for two hours, and following a strange note was definitely not part of his plan.

Still, he decided to check.

Under the clock, he found another note:

> **Good. Now find a resource that contains no words but can still give you information.**

Leo looked around. Books contained words, so the answer could not be a book. Then he noticed a large campus map on the wall.

Behind the map was a third note. This one gave him a simple math problem. Leo made a mistake on his first try, but instead of giving up, he checked his work carefully. On the second try, he solved it.

The answer was “27”.

He walked to shelf 27 and found a small box. Inside it was a card that said:

> **You have just spent twenty minutes solving problems, paying attention to details, and correcting mistakes. That is also studying.**

Leo laughed.

He had expected some kind of prize, but the message itself was the reward. He felt **relieved** because he had worried that he was only wasting time.

When he returned to his desk, he noticed something interesting: he could focus better than before. The short adventure had changed his mood.

Later, Leo wrote one sentence in his notebook:

> **A useful method does not always have to be boring.**

The next day, he put a new note inside the same book for another student.

## Useful Words
- curious
- hesitate
- resource
- contain
- shelf
- relieved
- focus
- method

# The Tea Test That Fooled Everyone

Mia's class decided to do a simple tea test.

Their teacher prepared two cups of tea. One cup had a beautiful **label** saying, “High-quality tea from a famous area.” The other cup had no label at all.

Most students expected the first tea to taste better.

They took a small **sip** from each cup and wrote down their opinions. Twelve students chose the tea with the beautiful label. Only four preferred the other one.

Then the teacher smiled and told them the truth.

The two cups contained exactly the same tea.

The students were surprised. The tea had been produced in the same place, processed in the same way, and poured from the same pot. The only difference was the label.

The teacher explained that our expectations can have an effect on what we think we experience. If we believe something has high quality, we may pay more attention to its good points.

Mia laughed and said, “So sometimes my brain tastes the tea before my mouth does.”

After class, she wrote in her notebook:

> **Before I choose, I should check the facts, not only my expectations.**

## Useful Words
- label
- sip
- quality
- contain
- produce
- process
- effect
- attention

# The First Result Was Wrong

Nina had to give a short class report about sleep. She opened her laptop, searched for “how much sleep students need,” and clicked the first result.

The page looked professional. It had a clean design, several charts, and a bold **headline** saying that five hours of sleep was enough for most young adults.

Nina was surprised. The claim sounded convenient because she often slept only five or six hours.

She almost copied the claim into her report.

Then she remembered something her teacher had said:

> “A confident sentence is not the same as reliable evidence.”

So Nina checked the **source**.

The website belonged to a company selling a drink that promised to keep people awake. That did not automatically make the information wrong, but it gave Nina a reason to be careful.

She searched again.

This time, she found several health organizations and research articles. Their explanations were not exactly the same, but they generally pointed in a different direction from the first website.

Nina became **suspicious** of the original claim.

She went back to the first page and looked more carefully. At the bottom, in very small text, she found a sentence explaining that the “study” had included only twelve people.

Twelve.

Nina laughed.

The charts had made the result look much stronger than it really was.

She decided to **verify** every important claim in her report by checking at least two reliable sources. The process took longer, but her final report became much better.

During her presentation, one classmate asked, “How do we know which research to trust?”

Nina answered:

“Maybe we should not trust research just because it looks scientific. We should ask who funded it, how the study was done, and whether other evidence supports the same result.”

After class, Nina wrote a new rule at the top of her notes:

> **Check the evidence before you believe the conclusion.**

The rule was simple, but it changed the way she read information online.

## Useful Words
- headline
- claim
- source
- evidence
- suspicious
- verify
- conclusion
- reliable

# The Umbrella Library

One rainy Monday, a university placed twenty yellow umbrellas beside the main gate.

A sign said:

> **Borrow one when you need it. Return it when you can. No payment.**

Students quickly gave the small project a nickname: “the umbrella library.”

At first, some teachers thought the umbrellas would disappear within a week. There was no machine to record names, no deposit, and no employee checking who took what.

The project depended almost completely on **trust**.

During the first storm, eighteen umbrellas were borrowed.

The next morning, sixteen came back.

Two were still missing.

A week later, something unexpected happened. There were twenty-three umbrellas at the gate.

Three students had added their own old umbrellas to the collection.

One student explained, “I borrowed one when I was completely **soaked**. It helped me, so I wanted to provide the same help to someone else.”

By the end of the month, the number changed almost every day, but the project continued.

Some umbrellas disappeared permanently. Others appeared without explanation.

The university finally decided not to control the system too strictly.

The simple idea worked because most people understood the rule without being forced to follow it.

Sometimes a useful campus resource does not need advanced technology.

It only needs people who are willing to return what they borrow.

## Useful Words
- borrow
- trust
- soaked
- permanently
- provide
- resource
- depend on
- useful

# The Phone Basket Experiment

At the beginning of the semester, Professor Lee noticed a simple problem.

Many students placed their phones beside their notebooks during class. They did not use them all the time, but every few minutes a screen would light up. Someone would check a message, then another student would do the same.

Professor Lee did not want to ban phones completely. Instead, she tried a small experiment.

She placed a basket near the door and told the class:

> “You may keep your phone, or you may leave it in the basket for the next forty minutes. The decision is yours.”

At first, only six students used the basket.

A week later, twelve did.

By the end of the month, more than half the class was leaving their phones there.

Several students said they felt more restless when the phone was on the desk, even if they never touched it. They kept wondering whether a new message had arrived.

Professor Lee then asked the class to compare two short quizzes: one completed with phones on the desk and one completed with phones in the basket.

The average score was slightly higher in the second condition.

The result did not prove that phones always reduce learning. The class was small, and many other factors could affect the scores.

Still, the experiment gave students something useful: a simple method they could test for themselves.

Her goal was not to create one perfect rule. It was to help students notice what affected their attention and make a better decision.

## Useful Words
- restless
- ban
- slightly
- condition
- compare
- average
- affect
- decision

# A Seat for Ten Minutes

A small café near a university tried an unusual idea.

One table beside the window had a sign:

> **Free seat for ten minutes. No purchase required.**

Anyone could sit there for ten minutes without buying anything.

The owner created the seat after noticing that students often entered the café just to escape heavy rain or extreme heat. Some looked embarrassed because they did not want to buy a drink.

At first, the owner worried that the idea would reduce business.

Instead, something different happened.

People respected the ten-minute rule. Some left after resting. Others decided to buy something after all. A few regular customers even paid for an extra drink and asked the café to give it to the next student who needed one.

The owner said the seat changed the environment of the café.

“It reminds people that not every useful thing has to produce money immediately,” she explained.

The idea was simple, but it made the café more popular with students.

## Useful Words
- purchase
- embarrassed
- respect a rule
- environment

# The Ten Minute Check

At the end of a busy afternoon, Mei was ready to send her group's report to a teacher. The sentences looked clear, the pictures were bright, and the final page offered three useful suggestions. She wanted to finish the task and go home.

Then her friend Bo asked a simple question. "Have we checked the number on the first page?"

Mei looked at it. The report said that their small reading room had received five hundred visitors in one day. That sounded impressive. However, the room had only twenty seats.

"An online tool gave me that number," she explained. "It was in the first answer."

Bo did not say that the answer was wrong. He asked to see the source. They opened the library's own page and found a detail they had missed. Five hundred was the total for a whole week, rather than a single day.

Mei felt embarrassed. Her face grew warm, and she wished she had checked earlier. "We need to rewrite everything," she said.

"Not everything," Bo replied. "Let's make a short checklist."

They wrote down three things: numbers, sources, and sentences. Mei checked the information while Bo reviewed the language. They noticed two small mistakes and corrected them. The rest of their work could stay.

Ten minutes later, the report was ready. It was not longer or more difficult to read. It was simply more accurate.

Before sending it, Mei added one final note: "Visitor numbers are based on the library's weekly record."

As they left, she noticed the receipt from her afternoon snack in her pocket. She always checked the price before paying. Why had she trusted a surprising number without checking it?

The next morning, Mei put their checklist beside her laptop. It was a small reminder: useful tools can save time, but a careful person still needs to ask where an answer comes from.

## Useful Words

- checklist — 检查清单
- accurate — 准确的
- be based on — 基于
- embarrassed — 尴尬的
- receipt — 收据
- reminder — 提醒物

# The Lamp at the Repair Table

Jun wanted to buy a new desk lamp, but his old one still worked. It simply leaned to one side. Before spending money, he took it to a repair table in the campus library.

A volunteer named Lin looked at the lamp and asked Jun to hold its base. She picked up a screwdriver, a tool for turning small metal screws. After a few careful turns, the lamp stood straight again.

"That was quick," Jun said. "I thought we would need a new part."

Lin smiled. "Sometimes the problem is smaller than it looks. But we have to check before we decide."

Jun asked how much he should pay. The repair table was free. Instead, Lin asked him to explain the repair to the next student waiting in line.

At first, Jun hesitated. He paused because he was not sure he could explain it clearly. Then he pointed to the loose screw, which had not been holding the lamp firmly, and showed how to turn it.

The student understood. Jun left with a useful lamp and a little more confidence. He had saved money, but he had also learned something: receiving help can be the first step toward helping someone else.

## Useful Words

- lean — 倾斜
- screwdriver — 螺丝刀
- hesitate — 犹豫
- loose — 松动的
- explain something to someone — 向某人解释某事
